### **Article 1: The Birth of an Association: UONAA Launched**

Chapter: Central Date: February 5, 2005   
On Saturday, 5th February 2005, the University of Nairobi Alumni Association (UONAA) was officially launched at a colourful ceremony. The event, held at the Chancellor's Court, was presided over by the then Minister for Education, Science and Technology, the late Prof. George Saitoti, EGH. The launch was attended by Kenya's top personalities in education and the public and private sectors.

The journey to this milestone had begun in earnest in September 2004, when the University Council formally requested Dr. Betty Gikonyo, an alumna and member of the Council, to lead the way in establishing the association. Dr. Gikonyo, who had served on the University Council since 2001, accepted the challenge to chair a sub-committee on the launch. The sub-committee's first task was to draw up the association's objectives and draft a constitution. A critical step was obtaining formal permission to use the protected names "University" and "Nairobi", safeguarded by an Act of Parliament. Once this was secured, the registration process was successfully completed.

The launch committee then organised a breakfast meeting with the media to sensitise the country on the University of Nairobi's contributions to Kenya's development. The committee approached a few graduates to act as interim officials on a voluntary basis. The culmination of these efforts was the launch ceremony, where Prof. Saitoti congratulated the alumni for taking the bold step. He appealed to UoN alumni to give total support to the association, adding that the government viewed alumni associations as a positive synergy that could be tapped for the benefit of institutions and society. He reminded the guests that the best universities worldwide had strong and vibrant alumni associations, a practice to be emulated by Kenyans.

The then Chancellor, Dr. Joseph Barrage Wanjui, pledged to support the association, adding that the involvement of alumni in the governance and development of the institution was critical in today's dynamic world. The alumni present were obviously happy to be celebrating the birth of what one observer called "the overdue baby" as hearty handshakes and exuberant hugs dominated the event.

**Article 2: Prof. David Wasawo: The Visionary Behind UONAA**  
Chapter: Central Date: February 10, 2005 Is Feature: 

Professor David Peter Simon Wasawo (17 May 1923 – 4 February 2014\) is widely recognised  
as the intellectual father of the University of Nairobi Alumni Association. The association was  
established in 2004 under his leadership, with Prof. Wasawo serving as the founding  
Chairperson of the Interim Committee.  
A distinguished zoologist and conservationist, Prof. Wasawo was a pioneer African zoologist in  
East Africa. He taught at Makerere University in the 1950s and 1960s before joining the  
administration of the University of Nairobi in 1965, serving as its first Deputy Principal from 1965  
to 1970\. He later joined international bodies, including UN agencies.

In 2003, the University Council invited him to lead the Taskforce on Alumni Relations, which  
produced the blueprint for the association. The taskforce consisted of members who crafted the  
framework that would guide the alumni body. Prof. Wasawo insisted that the association must  
be independent of university administration, funded by membership fees, and governed by  
elected alumni. This model was adopted unanimously. He also played a key role in drafting the  
association&\#39;s motto.

Despite his pivotal role, he declined to stand for election as chairperson due to age but  
remained a patron until his death in 2014\. In his keynote address at the UONAA 20th  
anniversary celebrations in 2025, University Council Chair Prof. Amukowa Anangwe paid tribute  
to the founding visionaries, including the late Dr. Joseph Barrage Wanjui and the late Prof.  
George Magoha, for laying the groundwork for an association that continues to transform lives.

**Article 3: How the Alumni Association Came to Be: The Council Decision**  
Chapter: Central Date: January 20, 2005 Is Feature

The legal foundation for the University of Nairobi Alumni Association was laid when the  
University Council repealed Statute XXVIII of the University of Nairobi Act. This statute had

governed the Convocation—the assembly of all graduates—but was deemed outdated because  
it did not allow for direct election of leadership or independent fundraising.

The new legal framework, drafted by the University Legal Office in consultation with the  
taskforce, created the Alumni Association as a separate legal entity, distinct from the  
Convocation. The Convocation was retained as a ceremonial body that meets once every five  
years to elect the Chancellor&\#39;s nominees, while the Alumni Association became the operational  
arm.

The resolution was passed unanimously, with one abstention. The Chancellor, Dr. Joseph  
Barrage Wanjui, noted: \&quot;This is a historic day. For 35 years, we have talked about engaging  
alumni; now we have the instrument.\&quot; The legal change was gazetted on 3rd February 2005,  
just two days before the public launch.

The decision to repeal the statute was informed by the recognition that alumni play an important  
role in the development of the institution. As Dr. Betty Gikonyo, who would become the  
association&\#39;s first Chairperson, later noted, it is in recognition of the important role the alumni  
play that the Council in 2004 repealed University Statutes XXVIII on convocation, which gave  
birth to the UONAA.

**Article 4: Dr. Betty Gikonyo: The Driving Force**

Chapter: Central Date: February 5, 2005 Is Feature: No \| Is Highlighted: Yes

Dr. Betty Gikonyo, a consultant cardiologist and alumna (MBChB 1976, MMed 1982), is the  
founding Chairperson of the University of Nairobi Alumni Association. She served as  
Chairperson from 2005 to 2015\.

The story of UONAA began when Dr. Gikonyo, an alumna member of the University Council,  
was challenged to chair a sub-committee on the launch of the alumni association. Her  
leadership style was described as \&quot;hands-on and impatient with bureaucracy.\&quot; She was known  
for her fundraising acumen.

Under her leadership, the association grew from a founding membership of 75 to over 16,000  
members. Between 2005 and 2015, the Alumni Association donated over eleven million shillings  
towards supporting bright and needy students achieve their academic endeavours.

In a speech read on her behalf by Dr. Cleopa Mailu during the University of Nairobi&\#39;s 47th  
graduation ceremony, Dr. Gikonyo said it is in recognition of the important role the alumni play  
that the Council in 2004 repealed University Statutes XXVIII on convocation which gave birth to  
the UONAA. She noted that the proposed Universities (Higher Education) Bill 2012 further  
recognised alumni associations.

Her immediate priorities as Chairperson were: opening a bank account for the association,  
designing a membership database, launching a newsletter, and identifying the first cohort of  
scholarship beneficiaries. She continued to serve as an inspiration to the young and old alumni  
wishing to venture into the tricky and challenging business world.

**Article 5: Prof. George Magoha Role in Establishing UONAA**  
Chapter: Central Date: May 1, 2005 Is Feature: No \| Is Highlighted: No

Prof. George Albert Omore Magoha (1952–2023), who assumed office as the 7th Vice-  
Chancellor of the University of Nairobi on 1st May 2005, played a crucial role in the  
establishment and early success of the Alumni Association.

The University administration, led by the then Vice-Chancellor Prof. George Magoha, agreed to  
support the association until it could run independently. Prof. Magoha also mandated officers  
from the University to work with the association.

Prof. Magoha was an alumnus of the university (MBChB 1976, MMed 1981\) and had previously  
served as Dean of the School of Medicine (1996–2000). He was the first Vice-Chancellor who  
was also a paid-up member of the newly formed Alumni Association.

His support was instrumental in weaning the new outfit, as it was clear from the beginning that  
the association would need to be supported for some time. The partnership between the  
University and the Alumni Association was set to grow as UONAA moved ahead in the  
realisation of its core projects, including the construction of an Alumni Centre, University  
scholarships, and an Endowment Fund.

At the UONAA 20th anniversary celebrations in 2025, University Council Chair Prof. Amukowa  
Anangwe paid tribute to the founding visionaries, including the late Prof. George Magoha, for  
laying the groundwork for an association that continues to transform lives.

**Article 6: UONAA Vision, Mission, and Core Values**  
Chapter: Central Date: February 5, 2005 Is Feature: No \| Is Highlighted: No

At its launch in February 2005, the University of Nairobi Alumni Association (UONAA)  
established a clear vision, mission, and set of core values to guide its activities.

Vision: To be a leader in promoting active, visible leadership in the community by fostering  
interaction among the alumni, the student community, family, friends, and industry.

Mission: To safeguard the best interest of its members and use their talents and resources to  
support the University&\#39;s quest of achieving international distinction in quality teaching, research,  
and service.

Core Values: Service, Professionalism, and Integrity.

The association brings together graduates of the University to participate in governance and  
development of their alma mater as they socialize and network. It provides a forum through  
which they participate in the promotion of an active and visible leadership role in the community.

The association focuses its activities around the student and has established an educational  
bursary fund, an annual academic prize award for the Best Overall student in each College, and  
the improvement of physical facilities.

**Article 8: UONAA Scholarship Fund: A Legacy of Giving**  
Chapter: Central Date: March 10, 2005 Is Feature

The UONAA Scholarship Fund was established shortly after the association&\#39;s launch. The fund  
provides University Scholarships and Mentorship to academically gifted but financially  
disadvantaged students undertaking their undergraduate courses at the University of Nairobi.

Since its inception in 2005, the Fund has supported over 3,000 needy and bright students to  
meet their educational needs. The association&\#39;s long-term objective is to create an endowment  
fund and spend the interest to sponsor needy students and establish chairs of research.

The fund was managed by a sub-committee chaired by Dr. Betty Gikonyo, with representation  
from the Dean of Students office. The fund&\#39;s mandate was to provide financial support and

mentorship to full-time undergraduate students who demonstrated academic excellence but  
faced financial hardship.

Between 2005 and 2015, the Alumni Association donated over eleven million shillings towards  
supporting bright and needy students achieve their academic endeavours. The Fund has been a  
cornerstone of the association&\#39;s identity and mission, embodying its commitment to giving back  
to the alma mater.

**Article 10: UONAA Registers as a Legal Entity**  
Chapter: Central Date: March 2, 2005 Is Feature: No \| Is Highlighted: No

Following the repeal of Statute XXVIII, the interim committee applied for registration under the  
Societies Act (Cap 108). The application was submitted to the Registrar of Societies on 15th  
February 2005, and approval was granted on 2nd March 2005\.

There were consultations between the University Legal Officer and the Registrar of Societies  
aimed at expediting the registration process. With this registration, the association could open

and operate bank accounts, own property and enter into contracts, receive tax-deductible  
donations, and sue and be sued in its own name.

The first banking signatories were Dr. Betty Gikonyo (Chairperson), Mr. Nathan Kahara (Vice-  
Chair), and Prof. Mabel Imbuga (Secretary). The initial account balance was transferred from  
the University Council&\#39;s seed grant.

The registration marked a significant milestone for the association, providing it with the legal  
standing necessary to operate independently and effectively.

**Article 11: UONAA First Membership Drive**  
Chapter: Central Date: March 15, 2005 Is Feature: No \| Is Highlighted: No

Following its launch, UONAA embarked on its first membership drive, aiming to register as  
many alumni as possible. The association&\#39;s initial database consisted of contact information  
compiled from various University records.

The membership drive utilised multiple channels: direct mail, email, and word-of-mouth through  
alumni networks. The association also benefited from a \&quot;bring a friend\&quot; campaign targeting the  
classes of 1970–1985, who were the most responsive.

The annual subscription fee was set at KSh 1,000 (approximately USD 13\) for local members  
and USD 20 for diaspora members. By the end of 2005, the association had registered 2,147  
paid-up members.

The membership drive was supported by the University administration, which provided office  
space and administrative assistance. The success of the drive demonstrated the strong demand  
among graduates to stay connected with their alma mater.

In 2007, the University of Nairobi established state-of-the-art level 3 laboratories to handle  
highly contagious pathogens. These facilities were part of a broader effort to boost infectious  
disease research in East Africa.

highly pathogenic microorganisms. Keith Fowke of the University of Manitoba said the facility  
would provide \&quot;a cutting-edge HIV research laboratory capable of performing studies on  
virology, immunology and genetics right here in East Africa, in the heart of the HIV epidemic\&quot;.  
The lab was one of only three advanced facilities for diagnosing haemorrhagic fever in Africa,  
with others located in Gabon and South Africa.

**Article 4: Prof. David Wasawo Passes Away**

Chapter: Central Date: February 4, 2014 Is Feature: No \| Is Highlighted: Yes

Professor David P. S. Wasawo, the intellectual father of the University of Nairobi Alumni  
Association, passed away on 4th February 2014 at the age of 91\.

Prof. Wasawo had served as the founding Chairperson of the Interim Committee that  
established the Alumni Association. He was a distinguished zoologist and conservationist, a  
pioneer African zoologist in East Africa. He taught at Makerere University in the 1950s and  
1960s before joining the administration of the University of Nairobi in 1965, serving as its first  
Deputy Principal from 1965 to 1970\.

In 2003, the University Council invited him to lead the Taskforce on Alumni Relations, which  
produced the blueprint for the association. He declined to stand for election as chairperson due  
to age but remained a patron until his death.

The University community mourned his loss and honoured his legacy as a visionary leader and  
dedicated servant of the institution.
